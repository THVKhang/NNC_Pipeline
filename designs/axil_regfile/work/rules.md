## Questions answered by the documents

- Does 'one cycle' latency mean the response is asserted in the next cycle after the handshake, and does it hold when bready/rready is low (stall)? Also what is 'last write handshake' when AW and W arrive at different times? → Yes. The last write handshake is Ec, the later of the AW and W transfers (or the shared edge). bvalid is 1 in the cycle that starts at Ec. Read data is valid in the cycle that starts at Ear. A low bready or rready does not delay this. It only keeps valid and the payload held until the B or R transfer. [1.2:s11, 3:s6, 7.4:s1, 7.4:s2, 7.5:s4, 7.5:s5]
- On an unmapped write, are register contents unchanged, and what happens with a partly mapped address range (addresses 0x00+4k beyond PARA_NUM_REGS up to 0x40)? → An unmapped write changes no register and answers SLVERR. Word indices PARA_NUM_REGS to 15 (the gap below 0x40) are unmapped, as are all word indices 17 and above. [7.1:s4, 7.1:s5, 7.3:s7, 7.4:s6, 10.1:s1, 9:s3]
- Does a write to the status register at 0x40 return SLVERR or OKAY? → OKAY. A write to the status register changes nothing and answers 2'b00. [7.4:s5, 10.1:s3, 9:s2, 9:s6]
- The quote for held read address is truncated; what are its exact conditions (ending on R transfer, reset)? → The spec defines it in full. A read address is held in cycle n if its AR transfer happened at an edge e <= n-1, its R transfer has not happened at any edge <= n-1, and i_rst_n has not been 0 since the AR transfer. [3:s11]
- Does the held state end if i_rst_n is 0 at the same edge as the B transfer, or only on a reset after the AW/W transfer? → A reset at any time since the AW or W transfer ends the held state. No transfer can happen at an edge where i_rst_n is 0, so a B transfer cannot occur at a reset edge. The reset discards the held state and the response. [3:s9, 3:s10, 6.2:s8, 6.2:s12]
- REQ-PAR-003 says the RTL 'should' stop elaboration on illegal parameters while REQ-PAR-004 says illegal behaviour is unspecified and not verified. Is the elaboration error required or optional, and is it to be checked? → The elaboration error is only recommended ('should'). Behaviour with illegal parameters is unspecified and is not verified, so the error is not checked. [4:s7, 4:s8, 14:s11, 1.3:s9]
- PARA_ADDR_W legal range 7 to 16 is justified by reaching the 0x40 status register, but with PARA_NUM_REGS up to 16 the register space size relative to 0x40 is unclear. Is there an address overlap at 16 registers (16*4=0x40)? → There is no overlap. 16 registers occupy 0x00 to 0x3C, and the status register is at 0x40 (word index 16). [10.2:s2, 9:s3, 3:s14, 3:s15, 7.1:s2, 7.1:s3]
- RF-5-04 says outputs may change when i_rst_n is asserted, which suggests asynchronous reset. Is reset asynchronous or synchronous? That decides whether outputs may change between clock edges on reset assertion. → Reset assertion is asynchronous. Release is synchronous to i_clk. Outputs therefore take their reset values on assertion without waiting for a clock edge. [6.2:s2, 6.2:s3, 1.2:s13, 5:s3, 6.2:s7]
- Can o_regs be reset asynchronously and does the 'every output' in REQ-RST-004 include o_regs? RF-6.2-01 and RF-6.2-04 use different wording. → Yes. o_regs is an output with reset value 0. REQ-RST-004 covers every output, so it includes o_regs. Flip-flops are reset asynchronously. [5:s23, 6.2:s6, 6.2:s7, 13:s4]
- Is the pending write state, with address held but data not yet received, discarded entirely, and may a partial write have already changed a register? → The held state is discarded and no register is written by it after release. A register changes only at Ec, when both address and data have been taken, so a transaction with only the address held has not changed any register. [6.2:s12, 6.2:s13, 7.3:s5, 7.3:s9]
- The quotes use R1, Ec, Eb, Ear, Er and 'held' without defining them. Are R1 the first edge after reset release and 'held' the period from the AW/W transfer until the edge after Eb? The signal-level checks for these rules depend on the answer. → The terms are defined. R1 is the first rising edge with i_rst_n at 1 after a reset period. Ec is the later of Ea and Ew. Eb, Ear and Er are the B, AR and R transfer edges. 'Held' runs from the transfer until the B (or R) transfer, with a reset also ending it. A held address or data is not held in the cycle after the B transfer, and awready and wready return to 1 in the cycle that starts at Eb. [3:s5, 3:s6, 3:s7, 3:s8, 3:s9, 3:s10, 3:s11, 3:s12, 8.1:s3]
- Which edge is Ec relative to the AW/W transfers (Ew)? Is it the edge after both are taken, and is bvalid asserted in that same cycle? REQ-WR-005, REQ-WR-010 and REQ-WR-020 leave this open. → Ec is the later of Ea and Ew, or the shared edge if both happen together. It is not the edge after. bvalid is 1 in the cycle that starts at Ec, and the register is written at Ec. [3:s6, 7.4:s1, 7.3:s1, 7.3:s3]
- Do the AW/W holding rules, which say no other transfer is taken until the edge after Eb, permit awready or wready to go high before the B handshake, or only after it? The ready=1 rules and the 'no other transfer' rules may conflict. → No conflict. awready and wready are 0 while the address or data is held. Held lasts until the B transfer. They return to 1 in the cycle that starts at Eb. They do not go high before the B handshake. [7.2:s2, 7.2:s4, 7.2:s6, 7.2:s7, 8.1:s3, 3:s9]
- Is the read of a register at the same edge as Ec the only race with a write? How does o_regs relate to the old-value rule when Ear equals Ec? → The spec covers all three orderings of Ear against Ec for the same register. Ear equal to Ec returns the old value. Ear after Ec returns the new value. Ear before Ec is unaffected by the write. o_regs shows the new value from the cycle that starts at Ec. The read returns the value in the cycle that ends at Ear, which is the old one. [7.6:s4, 7.6:s5, 7.6:s6, 7.6:s7, 7.5:s8, 7.3:s3]
- Can awready/wready/arready be 1 while a response is pending and bready/rready is 0? TIM-003 says readies do not wait for valids, but TIM-010/011 say no new transfer is taken. Is the ready deasserted in that state, as REQ-WR-001/RD-001 presumably require? → No. While a response is pending, the address or data (or read address) is held, so the readies are 0. The readies do not depend on the valids; they depend only on the held state. The two rule sets are consistent. [7.2:s2, 7.2:s4, 7.5:s2, 8.1:s12, 8.2:s1, 8.2:s2, 14:s2]
- STATUS 'sampled at Ear' appears garbled; at which event or clock edge is i_status sampled (e.g., AR handshake or R data cycle)? → i_status is sampled at Ear, the AR transfer edge. It is the value in the cycle that ends at Ear. It is then held until Er. Example 3 shows this. [1.2:s6, 7.5:s9, 7.5:s10, 14:s7, 8.3:s17, 8.3:s18]
- The legal range of PARA_NUM_REGS (max 16) and minimum PARA_ADDR_W (must be at least 7 to hold STATUS at 0x40) is not stated. → The ranges are stated. PARA_NUM_REGS is 1 to 16. PARA_ADDR_W is 7 to 16, and 7 is the minimum that reaches the status register at 0x40. [4:s1, 4:s2, 4:s5]
- Is the OKAY response timing for a wstrb=0 or status-register write identical to a normal write? Quotes say no register changes, but timing is not explicit for the status case. → Yes. Every write transaction has bvalid from Ec, with the normal write timing. A write with wstrb = 0 answers exactly like a strobed write to the same address and changes no register. A status write answers OKAY and changes nothing. [7.4:s1, 10.1:s5, 7.3:s8, 10.1:s3, 10.1:s4]
- Is the unmapped decision for an unaligned address made only on the word index (bits above [1:0]), so an unaligned address in the unmapped range still returns SLVERR? → Yes. Bits [1:0] have no effect and are not an error. The decode uses the word index, so an unaligned address in the unmapped range is still unmapped and answers SLVERR. [7.1:s1, 3:s13, 10.1:s6, 10.1:s7, 7.1:s4]
- What exactly do REQ-RST-004, REQ-RST-005 and REQ-RST-009 require? They are referenced but not quoted, so the expected reset behaviour cannot be checked. → They are quoted in the spec. RST-004: on assertion every output takes its reset value without waiting for a clock edge. RST-005: no transfer at an edge where i_rst_n is 0. RST-009: a reset discards held write address, write data, write response and read address, with no response and no write after release. [6.2:s7, 6.2:s8, 6.2:s12, 6.2:s13]
- CFG-BASE has PARA_NUM_REGS=8 (0x00-0x1C with 4-byte registers), so is the unmapped gap 0x20 to 0x3C an upper bound that depends on PARA_ADDR_W=8 (0x00-0xFC)? Should unmapped addresses above 0x3C also be covered? → Yes. With 8 registers 0x20 to 0x3C is unmapped, and 0x44 up to the top of the bus is also unmapped. CFG-BASE highlights the 0x20 to 0x3C gap. Addresses above 0x3C are covered by the register map and by the required unmapped-address scenarios. [11:s1, 9:s3, 9:s4, 9:s8, 12.4:s8]
- With PARA_NUM_REGS=16 and PARA_ADDR_W=7, are registers at 0x00-0x3C, and does 'no unmapped address below 0x44' mean 0x40-0x43 is also mapped? That seems to conflict with 16 registers ending at 0x3C. → With 16 registers, REG_0 to REG_15 are at 0x00 to 0x3C, and 0x40 is the status register. 'No unmapped address below 0x44' means 0x00 to 0x3C are registers and 0x40 is the status register. There is no conflict. [10.2:s2, 11:s4, 9:s3]
- Before the first reset assertion outputs are undefined, yet REQ-VER-006 drives reset from time zero; is any check expected in the window before the first clock edge? Also RF-12.1-09 vs REQ-VER-010 'every cycle'. → No check applies before the first reset assertion, and REQ-VER-010 is included in that. Reset is driven from time zero for at least 2 cycles, so checking is from reset on. 'Every cycle' in REQ-VER-010 applies after that point. [12.1:s9, 12.1:s10, 12.2:s4]
- Does the mid-cycle reset assertion (REQ-VER-021) conflict with REQ-VER-003/004 which say reset changes only away from rising edges and release is synchronous? Assertion may be asynchronous while release is synchronous. → No conflict. Assertion is asynchronous, so REQ-VER-021 asserts reset between clock edges. Release must be synchronous, so the testbench changes i_rst_n away from clock edges, which fits both. [6.2:s2, 6.2:s3, 12.1:s3, 12.1:s4, 12.3:s2]
- Is 'defined values on all inputs while i_rst_n is 1' consistent with valids being 0 or 1 during reset and payload being free to change after reset? → Yes, they are consistent. Inputs must be defined (no X or Z) while i_rst_n is 1. During reset the valids may be 0 or 1. A reset ends the stability obligation, so after R1 the manager may drop a valid or change a payload. A valid at 1 after R1 is a new request. [12.1:s7, 12.1:s8, 12.1:s11, 12.1:s12, 6.2:s14]
- D-001 says readies are 0 during reset and in the cycle ending at R1, then 1 from R1, but D-010 says readies return to 1 the cycle after B/R transfer. Is behavior after a reset mid-transaction consistent, and what exactly is R1 relative to reset deassertion? → The behaviour is consistent. The readies are 0 during reset and in the cycle that ends at R1, and are 1 from R1. A reset mid-transaction ends every held state, so the R1 rule applies after it. D-010 covers the normal return after a B or R transfer. R1 is the first rising edge with i_rst_n at 1 after reset. [14:s1, 14:s10, 14:s14, 3:s12, 6.2:s9, 6.2:s10, 6.2:s12]
- D-009 says bresp/rresp/rdata are 0 during reset; does reset include the cycle ending at R1, and are outputs before the first reset unchecked (D-015)? → bresp, rresp and rdata are 0 only while i_rst_n is 0. After release and before a response, their values are unspecified and not checked. The checker compares them only when the valid is 1 or i_rst_n is 0. Before the first reset assertion, nothing is checked. [6.2:s5, 14:s9, 12.2:s5, 12.1:s10, 14:s15]
