## Open questions (the designer decides: `nnc answer <design> <n> "<decision>" --by <name>`)

1. Does 'APB transfers are sequential' mean one APB access fully completes before the next starts, with no pipelining? Is a gap needed between transfers? (AB-5.4-02) — *The spec says APB transfers are sequential and that pipelined transactions are excluded, so there is no pipelining. It does not say whether an idle or gap cycle is required or allowed between consecutive APB transfers. The designer must specify this.*
   - **Decision** (Claude (delegated by GabrielHCMUT), 2026-11-08T05:05:57+00:00): No pipelining: one APB transfer (SETUP then ACCESS until PREADY=1) completes before the next SETUP starts. The specification neither requires nor forbids an idle cycle between transfers, so tests and assertions must accept both (APB allows SETUP right after a completed ACCESS).
2. What is the round-robin policy when only one of AR/AW is pending, and how is the arbitration turn updated after a grant? (AB-5.1-02) — *The spec states round-robin only for the case when both AR and AW are pending. It does not explicitly state the grant behavior when only one is pending. Nor does it state how the pointer is updated at grant time as opposed to completion. The designer must define these.*
   - **Decision** (Claude (delegated by GabrielHCMUT), 2026-11-08T05:05:57+00:00): When only one of AR/AW is pending it is granted (no wait for the other). The tie preference matters only when both are pending: AR wins the first tie after reset, and the preference flips after every completed transaction, tie or not (REQ-FUNC-003, answered question 4). Tests check the order only on ties.
3. Are the Parameter Description, Parameter values and Parameter unit comments required in a fixed order, and may other comments sit between them and the declaration? 'Immediately precede' leaves this open. (AB-8-02) — *The spec lists the three labels (Parameter Description, Parameter values, Parameter unit) and says they immediately precede the declaration. It does not say whether the order is mandatory, or whether other comments or blank lines may sit between them and the declaration.*
   - **Decision** (Claude (delegated by GabrielHCMUT), 2026-11-08T05:05:58+00:00): RTL coding-style rule (VLSIT RTL Design Rules): not observable at the ports; out of scope of simulation, listed as not verifiable by simulation (lint/review).
4. The spec says an invalid PARA_AXI_ID_W is an elaboration error. Should the checker use a specific mechanism, such as $error or a generate-time assertion, and what is the expected tool behaviour? (AB-8-03) — *The spec only says an invalid PARA_AXI_ID_W is an elaboration error. It does not specify the mechanism ($error, generate assertion, etc.) or the expected tool behavior. The designer must decide.*
   - **Decision** (Claude (delegated by GabrielHCMUT), 2026-11-08T05:05:58+00:00): Out of scope of simulation: one simulation elaborates one legal configuration. The elaboration error for an illegal PARA_AXI_ID_W is a static elaboration check (TEST for widths 1, 4, 16 is a separate build each), not verified by this run.

## Questions answered by the documents

- PPROT mapping policy is TBD. Is PPROT a direct bit-for-bit copy of AxPROT, or can system policy alter it? → PPROT is a direct copy of AxPROT with no translation. The bridge does no local privilege check. System policy is TBD, but it is enforced by the APB Completers and does not alter PPROT in the bridge. [3:s12, 9:s8, 6.2:s43, 18:s2]
- The error-propagation and defensive request rejection behaviour is not defined. Which conditions are rejected, and what response (SLVERR or DECERR) is returned? → A request is rejected if it has an unsupported AxSIZE (not 0/1/2), an unsupported or reserved burst or length, an AxCACHE other than 0000/0001, or a 4-KiB crossing. The response is SLVERR, never DECERR. No APB access is issued. A read returns ARLEN+1 R beats. A write drains its W beats and returns one B. [3:s9, 3:s13, 3:s19, 3:s21, 6.3:s1, 6.3:s2, 9:s4, 9:s6, 9:s7]
- What does a single global transaction active at a time mean for AXI read and write concurrency? Are simultaneous read and write requests serialized, and in what order? → At most one read or write transaction is active at a time. Reads and writes do not interleave. If AR and AW are both pending, they are serialized by round-robin grant. AR wins the first tie after reset, and the preference flips after each completed transaction. The owner keeps the bridge until its final R or B handshake. [3:s5, 3:s6, 5.4:s1, 10.4:s1, 10.4:s2]
- Does the round-robin preference flip after every completed transaction, including error/unsupported ones and ones that had no AR/AW tie? → Yes. The tie preference flips after each completed transaction. The spec makes no exception for rejected transactions or for transactions that had no tie. [3:s6]
- For a write rejected for AxCACHE, is it drained for AWLEN+1 W beats and answered with one B SLVERR like other unsupported writes? Likewise, does a rejected AxCACHE read return ARLEN+1 R beats? → Yes. An AxCACHE other than 0000/0001 makes the request unsupported. A rejected write drains AWLEN+1 W beats with no APB and returns one SLVERR B. A rejected read returns ARLEN+1 SLVERR R beats with RLAST on the final beat only, and issues no APB access. [3:s13, 3:s19, 3:s21, 6.3:s1, 6.3:s2, 10.3:s13, 20:s6]
- After a PSLVERR on one write beat, do the remaining beats still go to APB, with the error accumulated into the final BRESP? → Yes. Write PSLVERR accumulates. Later beats still execute on APB. The final BRESP is SLVERR after all declared W beats. [10.2:s7, 12:s4, 20:s9, 3:s20]
- Actor labels are unclear. The bridge is an AXI slave and an APB master. Which side does each rule refer to? → The bridge is an AXI4 Subordinate on the AXI side (the AXI Manager is the peer). It is an APB4 Manager/Requester on the APB side (APB Completers are the peers). Each rule follows from which interface its signals belong to. [2.3:s1, 2.3:s2, 6.1:s1, 6.1:s2, 21:s2, 21:s3]
- How should the 'no APB timeout' rule be verified given that it waits indefinitely, and how does it relate to the hold-while-PREADY=0 rule? → The bridge has no timeout. It holds the APB access while PREADY=0 and waits indefinitely. It is verified with wait-state assertions (TEST-003) that APB control/payload stays stable during the wait. Formal liveness assumes the APB eventually becomes ready. The two rules are the same behavior seen from two sides. [3:s23, 3:s15, 15.2:s3, 15.4:s1, 6.3:s3, 12:s5]
- AB-4-03 says the default ID width is 4 and AB-4-04 says ID width is elaboration-configurable. What range of ID widths is legal, and must verification cover widths other than 4 for CFG-BASE? → PARA_AXI_ID_W is legal from 1 to 16, with default 4, and an invalid value is an elaboration error. It sets both read and write ID widths. Verification must sweep widths 1, 4 and 16, not only 4. [3:s18, 8:s4, 8:s7, 15.2:s7]
- AB-4-01 says 'Full AXI4 Subordinate' but 'exclusive' and 'USER/QOS/REGION' are disabled in AB-4-02. What is the exact expected response to exclusive accesses and to USER/QOS/REGION signals in CFG-BASE? → The AxLOCK, USER, QOS and REGION ports are omitted, so no such signals exist to respond to. Exclusive access is not implemented, and integration must not send exclusive traffic. No exclusive response is defined because that traffic is outside the profile. [3:s12, 3:s14, 20:s5, 1.1:s5, 4:s1]
- What response does the bridge return for an illegal write count (the quote says 'legal write count is AWLEN+1')? How are illegal lengths handled? → Legal write count is AWLEN+1. An unsupported length or burst is rejected: no APB, drain the W beats, one SLVERR B. A W count that mismatches AWLEN, such as a WLAST mismatch, is a malformed AXI Manager violation. It is outside the guaranteed profile, with no RTL recovery or defined response. [5.4:s4, 3:s8, 3:s9, 6.3:s2, 6.2:s17, 20:s17, 20:s21, 12:s7]
- Is the 4-KiB crossing rejection applied to writes too? Only the read case is explicit in the table. If so, how does it interact with draining W and returning SLVERR B? → Yes, it applies to writes too. A 4-KiB crossing is rejected without APB for both reads and writes. A rejected write drains the declared W beats, makes no APB access, and returns one SLVERR B after the final W. [3:s9, 10.3:s14, 6.3:s6, 6.3:s7, 10.2:s4, 10.1:s5]
- How do AB-6.3-15 and AB-6.3-14 relate? One says AXI prohibits the crossing, and the other says the bridge rejects it. Is a crossing burst legal stimulus or outside the guaranteed profile? → AXI4 prohibits a crossing burst, so it is not legal AXI stimulus. The bridge defensively rejects it before any APB traffic, so its behavior is defined: SLVERR with no APB. It is a tested boundary case (WF-013), not part of the generic undefined malformed-AXI profile. [6.3:s6, 6.3:s7, 3:s9, 10.3:s14]
- AB-7.3-01 and AB-7.3-04: after reset, does the bridge return to a defined idle state, including AXI READY signals and the APB address and data? The quote only gives VALID=0 and PSEL/PENABLE=0. What may a testbench expect from an interrupted transfer, for example an AXI response that was already started or an APB access in progress? → Yes. Reset in any state returns to IDLE and clears local state. AXI VALIDs and READYs are 0 in reset. APB PSEL and PENABLE are 0, and PADDR, PWDATA and the other APB outputs are 0 (idle). An interrupted transfer is canceled. Completion or rollback of an in-flight AXI response or APB access is not guaranteed. [10.1:s6, 10.2:s5, 7.3:s1, 7.3:s4, 6.2:s14, 6.2:s19, 6.2:s32, 6.2:s39, 6.2:s44, 6.2:s45]
- AB-7.3-01: 'AXI VALID outputs must be 0 during reset' and 'PSEL/PENABLE=0 after the active reset edge' differ in timing. Is the reset value applied combinationally while reset is low, or only after the first clock edge in reset? → Reset is synchronous. It is sampled on the rising clock edge, with synchronous assertion. Outputs take their reset values (VALID=0, PSEL/PENABLE=0) after the clock edge that samples reset active, not combinationally on reset assertion. [7.3:s1, 7.3:s2, 3:s17, 6.2:s5]
- Does the 'fixed at 32 bits' rule cover only the address, data and APB data widths, or also strobe or other widths? The quote doesn't list the signals. → Address, data and APB widths are fixed at 32 bits. Every other port width is fixed in the port list, for example WSTRB and PSTRB at 4 bits, LEN at 8 bits and PROT at 3 bits. Only the ID width is parameterized. [8:s6, 6.2:s16, 6.2:s42, 6.2:s8, 8:s4]
- Does 'reject' mean SLVERR on both read and write for 4-KiB crossing, bad AxBURST and bad AxCACHE, and is any APB transfer issued? → Yes. Reject means no APB access. A read returns ARLEN+1 R beats with SLVERR. A write drains the W beats and returns one SLVERR B. This applies to 4-KiB crossing, a reserved or unsupported AxBURST, and an AxCACHE other than 0000/0001. [3:s9, 3:s13, 3:s19, 3:s21, 6.3:s1, 6.3:s2, 9:s6, 9:s7]
- Who is the 'Manager' in the lane-related rules: the AXI master or the APB side? → The Manager is the AXI Manager, the source of AXI address, size and WSTRB. It must assert only legal lanes and must select the requested bytes from the bus lanes using the AXI address and size. The bridge passes WSTRB to PSTRB unchanged and applies no lane rotation. [9:s9, 9:s11, 9:s13, 9:s2, 20:s21]
- Does reject/unsupported profile definition (which burst/size/length are unsupported) need to be specified? Unsupported is only referenced as 'outside profile'. → Unsupported is defined as: AxSIZE not 0/1/2, a reserved AxBURST, or a length outside FIXED 1–16, INCR 1–256 or WRAP 2/4/8/16. It also covers AxCACHE other than 0000/0001 and 4-KiB crossing. Each is handled by the REJECT_READ or REJECT_WRITE path. [3:s8, 3:s9, 3:s13, 9:s4, 9:s6, 9:s7, 10.1:s4, 10.1:s5]
- Is round-robin arbitration state defined after reset and does the last-granted pointer reset to a defined value? → Yes. Reset discards local state, and AR is chosen on the first tie after reset. The preference then flips after each completed transaction. [3:s6, 10.1:s6]
- In REJECT_WRITE, is B issued only after the final W (10.2-04) and does AW/W ordering allow W before AW? → Yes. In REJECT_WRITE the bridge drains the declared W count with no APB, and B (SLVERR) comes only after the final W. W may arrive before AW, but WREADY stays low until AW is accepted. [10.2:s4, 10.1:s5, 5.3:s3, 6.3:s2]
- What exactly does 'Reset cancels' mean for each error (flush of outstanding R/B beats, APB abort)? Is the mid-transfer APB state and AXI handshake state after reset defined? → Reset cancels in-flight local state: outstanding R/B work is discarded and APB is deselected. Outputs go to the reset values (VALID=0, PSEL/PENABLE=0). Completion or rollback of an interrupted transfer is not guaranteed. The SoC coordinates reset with the AXI Manager and APB fabric. [7.3:s4, 10.2:s5, 10.1:s6, 7.3:s1, 7.3:s3]
- ERR-006: does the bridge itself detect unmapped addresses, or does the Default Completer in the interconnect drive PSLVERR so that it reduces to ERR-003/ERR-004? Who is the actor? → The bridge does no address decode. The external default error Completer in the APB fabric drives PREADY=1/PSLVERR=1. The bridge then handles it as an ordinary PSLVERR, giving SLVERR on a read beat or accumulated into BRESP on a write. A missing default responder is an integration violation. [3:s22, 6.3:s5, 1.1:s5, 4:s4, 12:s6]
- ERR-005 'Reset deselects': does PSEL fall immediately upon reset assertion, and does that violate APB protocol mid-transfer? → Reset is sampled synchronously, so PSEL/PENABLE go to 0 after the active reset edge, not asynchronously. The in-flight transfer is canceled and its completion is not guaranteed. The SoC must coordinate the reset between the AXI Manager and the APB fabric. [7.3:s1, 7.3:s3, 7.3:s4, 10.2:s5, 12:s5]
- TEST-003 does not say which payload signals must stay stable during stalls (for example AWADDR, WDATA, PADDR, PWDATA, RDATA, RRESP), or the exact stall conditions. Define the signal set and the stall conditions. → Stability applies to the APB control and payload signals while PREADY=0: PADDR, PWRITE, PPROT, PWDATA, PSTRB, PSEL and PENABLE. It also applies to AXI VALID/payload through the handshake. The R payload (RID, RDATA, RRESP, RLAST) and B payload (BID, BRESP) stay stable while VALID=1 and READY=0. The stall conditions are APB PREADY=0, RREADY=0 and BREADY=0. [3:s15, 5.4:s2, 5.4:s5, 6.2:s39, 6.2:s41, 6.2:s42, 6.2:s20, 6.2:s33, 15.2:s3, 15.3:s1]
- TEST-001 does not define 'legal sizes' (for example the maximum size against the data width), nor the burst lengths and alignment. Define them so that stimulus and expected results can be written. → Legal sizes are AxSIZE 0/1/2 (1/2/4 bytes). Burst lengths are FIXED 1–16, INCR 1–256 and WRAP 2/4/8/16. The AXI address is byte-addressed, and APB PADDR is aligned down to the 4-byte word (bits [1:0] cleared). A burst must not cross a 4-KiB boundary. FIXED holds the address, and INCR and WRAP follow the AXI address rules. [3:s8, 3:s9, 3:s10, 9:s1, 9:s4, 10.2:s1]
- AB-18-03 does not say how errors propagate. Is it SLVERR/DECERR from PSLVERR to xRESP, and is the mapping the same for reads and writes? → APB PSLVERR maps to AXI SLVERR, never OKAY and not DECERR. A read marks the R beat of that transfer SLVERR. A write accumulates the error into the final BRESP=SLVERR. The mapping is the same value (SLVERR) for both, but the write error is aggregated across beats. [3:s20, 3:s24, 12:s8, 10.2:s6, 10.2:s7]
- AB-20-06 is marked as needing integration confirmation. Is the reset behavior (no replay or rollback) confirmed, and what is the expected state of in-flight AXI and APB transfers at reset? → The behavior is defined: reset cancels local state with no replay or rollback. In-flight AXI and APB transfers are canceled, and completion is not guaranteed. The assumption's status is still 'Integration confirmation needed', so it is not yet confirmed. [20:s18, 7.3:s4, 10.2:s5, 10.1:s6]
- AB-20-05 leaves APB side effects of malformed writes unspecified. Should verification avoid driving illegal WLAST/WSTRB traffic entirely? → Malformed writes (WLAST mismatch, illegal WSTRB lanes) are outside the guaranteed profile. The Manager must comply with AXI4, and no RTL recovery or expected behavior is defined. Verification therefore has nothing to check for such traffic and should keep stimulus AXI-legal. [20:s21, 20:s17, 6.2:s17, 9:s9, 12:s7]
- AB-20-07 is listed as TBD for SoC integration. Who owns the default-response decoder, and is it in scope for the testbench? → The decoder and default error Completer are external, in the SoC/APB fabric, and the SoC integrator owns them (address map, default PREADY=1/PSLVERR=1). They are outside the RTL. The testbench must still model the unmapped-address default response, since TEST-004 injects APB errors and unmapped addresses and REQ-ERR-004 is verified by integration test. [1.2:s5, 3:s22, 4:s4, 11.2:s2, 20:s22, 15.2:s4, 19:s19]
